5W1H - WHAT

Normal Competitive Banter vs Toxic Harassment

SLIDE 03 / 13
ACCEPTABLE PLAY Click to Flip ↻

Normal Competitive Banter

  • Playful teasing focused only on match skill or score.
  • Fast tactical callouts to coordinate team moves.
  • Mutual respect that stops when someone feels uncomfortable.
Boundary: Targets the game avatar - never the real person.
EXAMPLE Click to Flip Back ↻
“Nice try on mid lane! Watch the flank next round, GG!”

Keeps energy high while respecting teammates and opponents as human beings.

Outcome: Fun rivalry with healthy sportsmanship.
ETHICAL & LEGAL BREACH Click to Flip ↻

Toxic Harassment

  • Personal attacks on race, gender, religion or identity.
  • Deliberate sabotage (griefing), stalking or leaking private data.
  • Real-world threats of violence or encouraging self-harm.
Boundary: Attacks real human dignity and personal safety.
HARM EXAMPLE Click to Flip Back ↻
“Leave this game [slur], I found your real name and address!”

Crosses from gameplay into emotional abuse, intimidation and potential cybercrime.

Outcome: Psychological distress and real-life danger.

🔗 WAO Malaysia - Stalking & Harassment Report ↗
WHY - CYBERPSYCHOLOGY CAUSES
1. Online Disinhibition Effect (Suler, 2004)

Hiding behind anonymous usernames makes people act crueler than they ever would face to face.

2. ELO Frustration & Rank Pressure

Losing rank points triggers anger that players unfairly take out on teammates.

3. Mob Mentality in Squads

When one player mocks a teammate, others pile on because nobody feels individually blamed.

4. Misused Free Speech Excuse

Some gamers defend slurs as “normal gaming culture” while ignoring basic respect.

🔗 Prof. John Suler (2004) - PubMed Article ↗
DEPTH OF ANALYSIS

Advantages of Game Chat vs Toxic Risks & Impacts

SLIDE 06 / 13
POSITIVE SIDE

Advantages of In-Game Communication

1. Fast Team Strategy

Real-time voice callouts help squads coordinate attacks and react quickly during competitive matches.

2. Global Friendships

Connects students with friends and teammates across different cities and countries.

3. Teamwork & Leadership Skills

Healthy lobbies encourage clear communication, problem solving and calm leadership under pressure.

Key Insight: Game chat has real social value - the goal is stopping abuse, not banning games.
RISKS & IMPACTS

When Chat Turns Toxic - Risks & Impacts

Hostile verbal abuse during gaming triggers real emotional stress, anxiety and drives players away.
  • Emotional Stress: Triggers anxiety, low self-esteem and fear of speaking in voice chat.
  • Players Quitting: Good players mute comms or abandon games - shrinking healthy communities.
  • Real-World Threats: Online fights escalate into doxxing, cyberstalking and physical harm.
Critical Reality: Unchecked toxicity destroys the teamwork benefits gaming aims to create.

Islamic Perspective & Malaysian Cyber Laws

SLIDE 09 / 13
ISLAMIC ETHICS (CHAPTER 2) Click to toggle
Surah Al-Baqarah (2:83) commands us to “speak kindly to people.” Cursing, mocking and insulting teammates in game chat directly break Islamic manners (Adab).
No harm should be caused or returned in online spaces. Even when another player provokes you first, replying with abuse is still ethically wrong.
Game creators and platform owners hold a moral trust (Amanah) to protect users - especially children and youth - from digital abuse.
MALAYSIAN CYBER LAW Click to toggle
MALAYSIAN CYBER LAW - ACT 588 & PENAL CODE
CMA 1998 (Act 588) & Penal Code Section 507A protect online safety in Malaysia.
Sending grossly offensive or menacing messages online with intent to harass others is a crime under Malaysian communications law - carrying heavy fines and possible jail time.
Repeated online stalking or harassment that causes fear or distress is punishable by up to 3 years in jail, a fine or both.

Realistic Solutions: IT & Non-IT Approach

SLIDE 10 / 13
Software alone is not enough - we need both technology and people.

Smarter Game Systems

1. Real-Time AI Moderation

Detect toxic voice and text chat immediately before abuse gets worse.

2. Phone Number Verification (2FA)

Stop banned toxic players from creating endless new free “smurf” accounts.

3. Reputation Matchmaking

Group polite players together and isolate repeat toxic players into separate lobbies.

People, Culture & Law

1. Digital Ethics Education (MCMC Klik Dengan Bijak)

Teach online manners and digital safety early in schools and universities.

2. Parent & Community Rules

Active parental supervision for kids plus zero-tolerance rules by streamers and esports clubs.

3. Legal Action & Victim Support

Clear reporting pipelines to MCMC, PDRM and Cyber999 plus mental health support for victims.

BACKUP Q1 • LAW

“In Malaysia, can a gamer really face legal action just for harassing or threatening someone in game chat?”

Yes. Under CMA 1998 Section 233 (Act 588), sending grossly offensive or menacing messages online to harass others is a criminal offense carrying heavy fines and possible jail time. Repeated online stalking also falls under Penal Code Section 507A (up to 3 years jail).
BACKUP Q2 - FREE SPEECH

“Does muting or banning toxic players violate their right to freedom of speech?”

No. Under Social Contract Theory, players accept a private game’s Terms of Service (ToS) before joining servers. Both Malaysian law and Islamic ethics (La Darar wa La Dirar) confirm that free expression never protects hate speech, harassment or threats.
BACKUP Q3 - SOLUTIONS

“Why do simple account bans fail without 2FA verification and digital ethics education?”

In free-to-play games, banned offenders create disposable “smurf” accounts within minutes. Combining phone-number 2FA, live AI tools like ToxMod (80%+ non-reoffending rate) and Non-IT education stops both repeat accounts and toxic habits at the root.
